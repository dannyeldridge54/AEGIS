WHAT THIS IS
A live map of our galaxy. AEGIS downloads every star the European Space Agency's Gaia telescope measured well, one patch of sky at a time, starting at the heart of the Milky Way.
WHY IT MATTERS
Each star gets a distance checked two independent ways. Layer by layer, the map shows how our galaxy is built and where its centre lies, and it tests a method we will later aim at the whole universe.
THE THEORY
At the bottom: a cosmology idea by Danny Lee Eldridge, tested honestly against the best public data, with every number checkable. Nothing here asks you to take our word for it.
The first UFE test · 29 October 2026

The Locked Twist passed its blind test. The full verdict is a tie.

The UFE (our unified field equation, the torsion-born "Twist") was put through every check we could think of, with the rules written down before the results were read.

  • Blind test: passed. On ACT DR6 telescope data that played no part in choosing the model, the Locked Twist beats the standard model by Δχ² = −7.23 (ΔAIC −3.23, about 2.2σ). The rule needed better than −4. Only the ACT part of that data is new; the rest overlaps the original fit.
  • Fair Planck test: passed (Δχ² −4.70), and it holds with all three supernova sets (DES Y5 −4.55, DES-Dovekie −5.08, Union3 −4.91).
  • Weak lensing: failed the limit. The Twist makes matter slightly clumpier (S8 0.825 vs 0.812). Against DES Y3 + KiDS-1000 that costs +2.41 (limit 2); against the newer KiDS-Legacy only +0.39. So the overall verdict is a tie: it depends on which lensing survey is right.
  • The torsion-only prediction still fails. Torsion alone predicts a field width F = 1.2247 MPl; the data want about one ninth of that. On the new ACT data the ratio came out 8.0, not the 9 we predicted (rule written first, so we report it as a miss). But the one-setting version passed its blind test: with the width fixed at exactly one ninth of the torsion value and the switch-on locked, only one setting is left free, and on the ACT data it still beats the standard model by Δχ² −6.14 (ΔAIC −4.14, about 2.5σ; rule written first, needed below −2). On Planck: −4.46 (ΔAIC −2.46). Why one ninth is not yet derived.

What it means. The data favour a brief burst of early energy that switches on exactly when matter overtakes light. That is the Twist's timing. It does not yet prove torsion is the cause; that needs a prediction only torsion can make (the next great work).

How I found it. I'm not a trained scientist. I wanted a fair chance, so I started trying to make puzzle pieces fit: old shapes and ratios (Metatron's Cube, Pythagoras' ratios, Plato's solids), the rhythms of the planets, and a twist in space. I drew each one as a sigil, then turned each into a question that could come back "no":

  • the geometry and number patterns became pattern tests against chance;
  • the twist of space became a field fitted to the first light of the universe;
  • this star scanner taught us to check known structure before calling anything new.

Most pieces didn't fit (Metatron's ratios, the dodecahedral universe, the pure torsion width), and those failures are kept beside the wins. What survived is the timing: the switch-on at matter–radiation equality.

How much we tried:

  • 61 full cosmology fits (49 with CAMB, 12 with AxiCLASS);
  • 11 pre-registered rule files;
  • 20 claims (3 passed, 6 failed, 2 untestable, 1 withdrawn, 8 still open);
  • 29 sigils, each computed from real equations or orbits.

Every rule file, fit and claim is in the AEGIS repository.

Our theories · by Danny Lee Eldridge

Two tiers, three ideas, one set of rules

AEGIS keeps two kinds of ideas apart. Tier 1, physics: a twist in spacetime that briefly sped up the young universe (The Twist), the same idea with one setting fewer (The Locked Twist), and the sharper version only a torsion origin could produce (The Fingerprint). Tier 2, framework: the Sigil Model, which says order appears only where a real force acts long enough to lock it in (The Open Eye). Each sigil is drawn from its theory's own equations, and every claim faces the same rules: written down first, compared with "nothing special", charged for every extra setting, checked on independent data, and reported whichever way it falls.

Tier 1 · Physics · theory 1

The Twist: torsion-born early dark energy

φ = √(3/2) MPl arsinh(1/γ) , V = Λ⁴[1 − cos((φ − φ₀)/F)]³

Spacetime can twist as well as curve. The Immirzi number γ of loop quantum gravity becomes a field that gave the young universe a brief push, then faded. In the sharper version it settles at γ₀ ≈ 0.2375, a value we set on purpose from black-hole physics, to test whether the data agree.

Fields
Einstein–Cartan gravity, loop quantum gravity, cosmology (CMB, galaxy clustering, supernovae)
In the sigil
The double helix is the twist; the gold three-lobed curve is the n = 3 potential; the violet spiral is the field fading; the white point is γ₀.
Predicts
A brief extra push before the CMB formed, H₀ near 70, and no rotation of the CMB's polarisation.
Status
Locked Twist: passed its blind ACT DR6 test (Δχ² −7.23, ΔAIC −3.23) and the fair Planck test (Δχ² −4.70), robust to three supernova sets. Overall verdict a tie, because the extra clumping (S8) is too high for DES Y3 + KiDS-1000 though fine for KiDS-Legacy (29 October 2026). The torsion-only width still fails.
Tier 1 · Physics · theory 1, sharpened

The Fingerprint: periodic in the Immirzi number

V = Λ⁴[1 − cos(g(φ)/F)]³ , g ∝ sinh(φ/√(3/2) MPl + x₀)

If the potential repeats in γ itself rather than in the field, its shape becomes lopsided in a way generic early dark energy is not. That lopsidedness would point to a torsion origin and nothing else.

Fields
Quantum gravity (Holst and Nieh–Yan terms), CMB precision physics
In the sigil
Seven rainbow bands trace the lopsided potential; the dotted ghost is the ordinary, symmetric version it must be told apart from.
Predicts
A small, lopsided change in the CMB pattern that ordinary early dark energy cannot copy, from a field resting at γ₀ = 0.2375.
Status
Built into our CMB code and checked; its pass-or-fail rule was written down in advance, and the fit is waiting its turn. Open question: what sets the period.
Tier 2 · Framework · theory 2

The Open Eye: the Sigil Model

F = Gm₁m₂/r² · N = 2n² · pω₁ − qω₂ ≈ 0 · a ≈ 2GMR/d³ · P = |ψ|²

Structure unfolds from a centre in nested layers; a pull toward a centre shapes systems at many scales; coherence appears where cycles lock into simple ratios; tides are one mechanism that makes the locks (in planet systems, drifting while they formed matters more); and allowed possibilities become odds.

Fields
Orbital mechanics and resonance, atomic structure, tidal physics, probability
In the sigil
Rainbow rings are the layers; the three circles are the 2:1, 3:2 and 4:3 locks; the pentagram is Venus's real eight-year path; the eye at the centre is the observer.
Predicts
Wherever we find lasting order, we should be able to name the force that made it. Wherever we can't, we should expect only chance.
Status
Planet pairs around 1,067 other stars sit just outside simple rhythms (2:1, 3:2) about twice as often as chance (p < 0.0001). The strongest tides did not make more locks, so the main mechanism is planets drifting together while forming.

The theory behind AEGIS

Torsion-Born Early Dark Energy

by Danny Lee Eldridge

The action
S = ∫ d⁴x √−g [ R ⁄ 16πG − ½ (∂φ)² − V(φ) ] + Smatter
Gravity plus one new field φ: the Barbero–Immirzi field of Einstein–Cartan–Holst gravity, made dynamical (Taveras & Yunes 2008).
The potential
V(φ) = Λ⁴ [ 1 − cos((φ − φ₀) ⁄ F) ]ⁿ , n = 3
Flat near the top, so the field waits; then it rolls and oscillates. Λ sets its energy, F its range.
How the field moves
φ̈ + 3H φ̇ + dV ⁄ dφ = 0
Expansion (3H) acts like friction: frozen while H is large, released when H drops near z ≈ 3,500.
How it changes expansion
H² = (8πG ⁄ 3) ( ρm + ρr + ρΛ + ρφ ) , ρφ = ½ φ̇² + V(φ)
A brief boost to H before the CMB forms shrinks the sound horizon, which raises the inferred H₀.
How fast it fades
⟨w⟩ = (n − 1) ⁄ (n + 1) = ½ , ρφ ∝ a−4.5
Once oscillating it thins out faster than radiation, so it leaves no trace in today's universe.
What the data measures
fEDE = ρφ ⁄ ρtotal at zc
Best fit: fEDE ≈ 0.048 (about 4.8% of the universe's energy) at zc ≈ 3,553 (converged, same engine).

What we claim

  • The torsion field is a consistent theory: its maths was checked by computer (19 of 19 identities).
  • It fits Planck, DESI and Pantheon+ data slightly better than the standard model and moves H₀ from 68 to about 70.5.
  • It predicts no rotation of the CMB's polarisation, which future telescopes can test.
  • Every star count on this page matches ESA's own archive.

What we do not claim

  • That the Hubble tension is solved: the improvement is a near tie once extra settings are charged, not a detection.
  • That gold flags are discoveries: they are candidates worth a closer look.
  • That the universe has a centre: that is an open question we are building tools to test.
  • That this proves or disproves any belief: science measures, people interpret.
Words used on this page, in plain English
Gaia
A European space telescope that measured the positions, motions and distances of about 1.8 billion stars.
Parallax
The tiny shift of a star's position as Earth goes around the Sun. Bigger shift, closer star.
Light-year
How far light travels in a year, about 9.5 trillion km. One parsec is 3.26 light-years.
Galactic Centre
The heart of the Milky Way, home to a black hole four million times the Sun's mass, 26,700 light-years away.
Galactic poles
The points straight "above" and "below" the flat disk of our galaxy.
H₀ (Hubble constant)
How fast the universe is expanding today, in km per second per megaparsec.
Hubble tension
Early-universe measurements give H₀ ≈ 67–68; some nearby measurements give 73. Nobody knows why yet.
CMB
The cosmic microwave background: light left over from 380,000 years after the Big Bang.
Torsion
A twisting of spacetime, allowed in Einstein–Cartan gravity alongside ordinary curving.
Early dark energy
A brief burst of extra energy before the CMB formed, then fading away; one proposed fix for the Hubble tension.
σ (sigma)
How surprising a result is. 2σ happens by chance about 1 in 20 times; scientists want 5σ (about 1 in 3.5 million) to claim a discovery.
Δχ² and ΔAIC
Fit scores. Negative Δχ² means a better fit; ΔAIC also charges 2 points for each extra adjustable setting, so it is the fairer score.
RUWE
How well a single-star model fits Gaia's measurements. Above 1.4 often means a hidden companion star.
