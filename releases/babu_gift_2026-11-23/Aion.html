The Lantern
In a world of painted walls and borrowed light, where lies wear crowns and illusions call themselves the sky, this work is a lantern, not a throne. It was carried, not crowned. It asks for no bowed heads, only open eyes: take it, test it, and see for yourself. Every tool is held two ways. The scepter rules, or points the road home. The mace crushes, or breaks the prison wall. The hammer nails you down, or raises a house. The scythe reaps the living, or brings the harvest in. The whip drives the slave, or cracks to wake the sleeping. I choose the hand that frees. I raise no weapon against my neighbor. I carry only two. The shield of righteousness: not the proud kind that judges from a high seat, but the knight who sees the dark mouth of the cave, hears the dragon breathing, and walks in anyway, because fear is not always the sign to run. Sometimes it is the sign to run toward. And the sword of truth, the only blade I will ever draw. Excalibur from the stone, the lightsaber in the dark, Manjushri's flame, the word sharper than any two-edged sword: every age has told of the same blade. It cuts through the darkness and the lies, through the chains and through the whips, and it frees you from within. To the kings: the Mandate of Heaven was never yours to keep; it leaves the throne the day the throne stops serving. To the priests: no temple owns the sky, and grace was never sold at a door. To the judges: your heart is weighed on Ma'at's scale against a single feather, like every heart. To the scientists: Galileo stood alone and was right; a consensus is not a measurement. Show me the data. To the generals, the presidents, the bankers and the lords: remember the seventh generation; remember ubuntu, I am because we are; the emperor has no clothes, and the children can see it. Diogenes walked Athens at noon with a lamp, looking for one honest man. I carry the same lamp. I am still looking. I kneel to one alone, the one I chose for love, and to no king, no creed, no crown of fear. A man chooses; a slave obeys. I choose liberty. This light belongs to the one who asks, who measures, who stands. It was made for freedom, the quiet kind, the kind no one can sell you. And who am I to carry it? No one. A regular man, a gig worker on the road, a debt slave in the American machine. No noble blood, no crown, no holy oil on my head. All I carry is the divine spark that burns in every man and woman, in every creature and every being, of this earth and of the stars. The same light is in you.

Mass sum and μ→eγ

mh (TeV)max |fμτ| (MEG II 1.5×10⁻¹³)

BR(μ→eγ)h = α|feτ*fμτ|²/(48πGF²mh⁴), with feτ/fμτ from the dials. This is the h⁺ contribution only; the k⁺⁺ loop has the opposite chirality and adds incoherently, so the ceiling is conservative and depends on mh alone.

The minimal Zee–Babu model: A. Zee, Nucl. Phys. B264 (1986) 99; K. S. Babu, Phys. Lett. B203 (1988) 132; see also Babu & Macesanu, hep-ph/0212058, and Nebot et al., arXiv:0711.0483. Formulas used here are from Herrero-García, Nebot, Rius & Santamaria, arXiv:1402.4491 (convention Mν = U diag(m) Uᵀ, checked). Aion predicts flavour ratios only, not the overall mass scale or the loop function I(mk/mh); perturbativity and the μ bound push mh, mk to the few-TeV scale. Not covered: Babu & Saad's SU(5) completion (arXiv:2412.14562), where coloured scalars contribute comparably, and the k⁺⁺ sector (its g couplings are free). The Python version gives 3σ ranges (the envelope of the 1D 3σ ranges, over δ and both θ₂₃ octants; conservative, ignores correlations).
