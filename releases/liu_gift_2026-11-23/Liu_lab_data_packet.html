1. What this is, in one paragraph

Numbers you can drop straight into your own single-mode model, computed with your published Hamiltonians and parameters. Everything was pre-registered (SHA-256 locked) before it was computed, checked by an independent referee, and corrected where the referee found problems. Section 7 lists what was withdrawn. Please rerun it all on your own machines; the code is in engine/.

2. Models used (your notation)

(a) Undriven spin mixing (Hardesty-Shaw et al., arXiv:2301.02707, sSMA): mean-field energy per atom with M = 0,
E/N = c2ρ0[(1−ρ0) + (1−ρ0)cosθ] + q(1−ρ0),   θ = φ+1+φ−1−2φ0.

(b) Driven, static Floquet limit (Austin-Harris et al., arXiv:2410.09015, Supplement Eq. S9, qB→∞):
H = (hc2/N)[N̂0(N−N̂0) + ½(N̂+−N̂−)²] − (hp⊥/2)Ŝy, solved exactly in the full spin-1 single-mode Fock space (dimension (N+1)(N+2)/2). First-order Floquet corrections (∝ c2/2qB, p/2qB) are not included.

To regenerate or change any parameter: engine/d2185_lib.py (exact Fock-space model) and engine/loom_qsim.py (mean field, exact SMA, Bose–Hubbard chain), with the drivers that made each file.

7. Honest limits and what the referee removed

  • Withdrawn: an atom-number threshold for revivals (an artefact of a fixed fidelity cut), and a claim that our variance results explain your 2024 variance growth (our width saturates within about 57 ms at small N, so it is not comparable).
  • Not included: first-order Floquet terms, trap inhomogeneity, Mott shells, lattice-depth → (J, U) mapping, atom loss, finite temperature, detection noise.
  • Small-N exact results are not your N ≈ 105 experiment; they speak to per-site occupations in a lattice.
  • Our reproduction of your 2023 numbers recovers your own fit; it is a calibration of our tool, not new physics.
