# Abstract

**Janus Twist theory** is the overall framework: one hidden shape faces both ways in time — toward the beginning (inflation) and toward the end state (Λ) — and the data choose between two paths, **Kairos** and **Chronos**. Its fully symmetric realisation is the model **SERAPIS**.

We collect a day of pre-registered tests (AEGIS D-notes D436–D521) into one picture: the inflaton and the dark-energy field are the same object, the **shape of a hidden two-torus**. While the torus is highly stretched its potential is a nearly flat plateau and drives inflation; it then relaxes to the most symmetric (square) shape, where the leftover vacuum energy is the cosmological constant Λ. The construction is built from modular (SL(2,ℤ)) functions of the torus shape, so the square torus τ = i and the hexagonal torus τ = ρ are automatic special points; in the fully modular form (**SERAPIS**) the square torus is a heavy vacuum (mass ≈ 4×10¹³ GeV, no fifth force) and the hexagonal point is a wall. The Standard Model is assumed to live on branes that do not wrap this torus and to feel it only through a heavy intermediate modulus ("lens"), which suppresses its matter coupling below 10⁻²⁸.

The data split the family into two branches. **SERAPIS–Kairos** (no fitted tilt; one torus) predicts n_s ≈ 0.963–0.965 and r ≈ 0.0012–0.0013 once its own reheating is included: consistent with CMB-SPA alone (−1.1σ to −1.8σ) but 2.8–3.7σ from CMB-SPA+DESI. **SERAPIS–Chronos** (one fitted tilt) gives n_s = 0.9736 with r ≈ 0.0020 in its native SERAPIS form (0.0044–0.0046 in the earlier Twist-M/Route-A forms), consistent with both. Whether DESI's pull on n_s is real therefore selects the branch; n_s measured to ±0.002 is the main discriminator; r separates the native forms only at ≈1σ. The inflationary building blocks have clear prior art in modular-invariant inflation, which we credit; the contribution here is the single chain from inflation to Λ through one torus shape, its two-branch prediction, and the audit trail.

# 1. The picture

A two-torus has a shape τ in the upper half plane; tori related by SL(2,ℤ) are the same torus. In our variables T = −iτ, x = Re T = Im τ.

* **Stretched torus (Im τ ≫ 1): inflation.** The potential approaches a constant plateau (an α-attractor-type cusp plateau).
* **Relaxation: reheating.** The shape rolls toward the square and its oscillations reheat the universe; nucleosynthesis, acoustic oscillations, recombination and structure formation follow as in ΛCDM.
* **Square torus (τ = i): today.** The fixed point of τ → −1/τ; the vacuum energy there is Λ.
* **Hexagonal torus (τ = ρ = e^(iπ/3)):** the other fixed point; in SERAPIS a wall the shape cannot occupy.

Lattice symmetry explains why only these shapes are special: a 2D lattice admits only 1-, 2-, 3-, 4- and 6-fold rotations (crystallographic restriction; computed, D514), and both special shapes lie on the self-dual circle |τ|² = 1.

# 2. The model

**Kähler potential and superpotential.** One torus: K = −ln(2 Im τ); with a nilpotent stabiliser S, W = S·F(τ) and V = e^K|F|². Modular invariance requires F of weight −1, F = η(τ)^−2 H(j). We take (D517)

H(j) = (j − 1728)^(1/2) j^(−7/12) [ln(j/1728) + κ]^(1/2) + c·j^(−7/12),  κ = 1.

The factor j^(−1/12) cancels |η|^(−4) on the cusp and [ln j]^(1/2) cancels 1/(2 Im τ), giving the plateau; (j − 1728)^(1/2) ∝ (τ − i) makes the square torus a quadratic (stiff) minimum; j^(−7/12) makes τ = ρ a wall; c sets V(i) = Λ. A toolkit check gives j(i) = 1728, j(ρ) = 0 and SL(2,ℤ) invariance of ln V to 4×10⁻¹⁴ (D515).

**Λ.** Fixing V(i)/V_* = Λ/V_* requires c = 1.6×10⁻⁵³, i.e. an action S = −ln c ≈ 121.6 = 2π × 19.4: instanton-sized, though its geometric origin is not derived here (D517).

**Vacuum.** At 200-digit precision the square torus is a minimum in both directions with mass m ≈ 4.0×10¹³ GeV ≈ 5 H_inf (D517): no fifth force, no cosmological-moduli problem, no need for a trapping mechanism.

# 4. Matter and the extra dimensions

* **Lens (D505).** If matter couples to the torus only through a heavy modulus B with a mixing μ²ΔAΔB, stability (μ⁴ < m_A²m_B²) forces β_eff < β_B m_A/m_B — the coupling can only be diluted. In the Kairos vacuum the shape is heavy anyway, so the lens is needed mainly for lighter variants.
* **Where the torus lives (D508, DEEP 2.1b).** Exact SL(2,ℤ) on a single torus is natural for the complex-structure modulus of a factorised T⁶ = T²×T²×T² in IIB orientifolds, or the volume modulus of heterotic orbifolds; the Standard Model on branes that do not wrap that T² would be blind to it at leading order. A concrete compactification with these properties is not constructed here.
* **Earlier realisation (Twist-2, D436–D489).** A rolling Kähler modulus captured by a stringy (Π-stability) Fayet–Iliopoulos term on a magnetised D7-brane: capture works for string scales 3×10⁻⁵–0.3 M_P and soft masses 1 eV–100 TeV (D446, D452, D460, D483); the screening field's remnant is removed by hidden-sector annihilation (D447, D455, D468); it requires baryon number protection (D469), gauged DM stability (D473) and a fibred Calabi–Yau (D462). SERAPIS removes the need for capture by making the end point a symmetry-fixed heavy minimum.

# 5. Predictions and tests

1. **n_s** to ±0.002 (Simons Observatory, CMB-S4): Kairos 0.963–0.965 vs Chronos 0.9736.
2. **r** to ±0.001 (LiteBIRD, CMB-S4): Kairos ≈ 0.0012 vs native Chronos ≈ 0.0020 (≈1σ apart); the earlier Chronos forms (≈ 0.0045) would be separated at ≈3σ.
3. **w₀ = −1**, w_a = 0 (DESI DR3+, Euclid); a confirmed w₀ > −1 disfavours both branches.
4. **ΔN_eff ≈ 0.05–0.08** in the realisations with a light gravitino (D463, D511): a 2σ-level hint at CMB-S4.
5. **Cosmic birefringence tomography** for a separate axion (D484): Δβ = β_rec − β_reio up to 0.28° (≈5.5σ at LiteBIRD's headline noise) if the axion is at the heavy end of its range.
