The Weave

One thread, woven through every dimension, by every people. A physics idea about how the universe began, named with words from cultures around the world. We need your help to name it well.

What it is

The Weave is a proposal about cosmic inflation, the burst of expansion at the very start of the universe. It treats the strength of string interactions as a single thread that runs through every dimension of string theory. The energy that drove inflation comes from how fast the smallest possible black holes change size along that thread. The idea predicts two numbers that telescopes measure: the tilt of the early fluctuations (ns ≈ 0.971) and the strength of primordial gravitational waves (r ≈ 0.0005, or 0.001 if the universe passed through a six-dimensional phase).

SERAPIS-Λ: V = V∞ · ( ⅔ · y ∂ᵧ ln E(τ, 3/2) )² · [ 1 + C · ( E(τ, 3/2) − E(ρ, 3/2) ) ] E = 2ζ(3) y^(3/2) + 4ζ(2) y^(−1/2) + …, ρ = e^(iπ/3)

This is a draft by an independent researcher. It has not been peer reviewed. One step is assumed rather than derived, and the draft paper says so.

Help us weave it

We welcome anyone who speaks these languages or belongs to these traditions. Please write to us with any of these:

  • a translation of the one line into your language, with how to say it;
  • whether a name from your tradition is suitable, and a better one if it is not;
  • a name from a tradition that is not here yet, for a part of the theory;
  • how you would like to be credited.

Not yet represented, and warmly invited: First Nations of North America, Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander peoples, Arctic peoples, the Sámi, the Caribbean, the Horn of Africa, the Caucasus, Southeast Asia, and every other people. New names will be chosen together with people from those communities, not for them.

How the names are used

  1. Each name honours its tradition and labels a real part with a real meaning. None is used as a joke, product or logo.
  2. Names from living traditions are reviewed by people from those traditions before final use, and changed if they ask.
  3. Every name will carry its culture, an origin note and a pronunciation guide, written with the people who gave them.
  4. The physics stays neutral. The equations and numbers are the same for everyone, and anyone can check them.
