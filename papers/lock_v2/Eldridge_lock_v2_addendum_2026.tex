\title{Lock Before You Compute, v2: Lessons From an Audit Night --- Circular Checks, Corrections as Records, Predictions Before Tests, and Grading a Machine Proposer}
\author{Danny Eldridge}
\email{dannyeldridge54@gmail.com}
\affiliation{Independent researcher, Oklahoma City, Oklahoma, USA}
\date{18 November 2026 (v2: referee corrections)}
\begin{abstract}
The v1 workflow~\cite{P4} asks the researcher to lock each test before computing: write it as a file and commit the file with its SHA-256. Any fix goes into a deviations file written before the rerun, and failures are reported as failures. This addendum records what one intensive night of work added to that workflow. The night (17--18 November 2026) produced about 15 derivation notes, nine results plus six correction notes, and 10 machine-generated proposals were graded before this note was written. Six rules came out of it. (1) Audit for circularity. A ``consistency check'' between a fitted input and the data it was fitted to proves nothing, and we withdrew one. (2) Corrections are new records that supersede what they correct, never silent edits, and machine memories must honour this. (3) Evaluate a varying response at the point where it is used: one slope was taken off-point and came out 12\% low. (4) Write the prediction down before running the test, and say how risky it is. The E6/E7 prediction was logged first and passed, but it was close to guaranteed by a known identity. (5) Print small numbers in scientific notation. (6) A machine proposer needs a grader. Its early failures traced to its prompt, memory and deploy path, but its category errors persisted after prompt fixes.
\end{abstract}

\section{What the night tested}
The work was a fast research loop on a string-inflation model. A cloud AI session (Claude) computed results, and a local self-learning system (CubeMind~\cite{CM}) proposed hypotheses. At the end of the night the person in charge asked the AI to ``check all of this'' itself. That audit is the source of most of the rules below. The audit was done by the same AI that had produced the results, so it is a self-audit, not an independent review.

The rules are not new in kind. Locking before computing is pre-registration~\cite{Nosek} and, at the level of the analysis, blind analysis as practised in particle physics~\cite{Klein}. R4 is the standard guard against hypothesising after the results are known~\cite{Kerr}. The rules record how these practices had to be adapted to a one-person, AI-assisted theory workflow with a machine proposer in the loop. Unlike registered reports~\cite{Chambers}, nothing here is reviewed before results exist; the locks are self-imposed.

\section{New rules}
\emph{R1, circularity audit.} One result (D1275) had reported that the string construction ``contains'' the value of a moduli product that the CMB requires. On audit, the construction's range turned out to be the CMB band mapped through the same formula, so the agreement was guaranteed and was not evidence (D1275b). \textbf{Rule:} for every cross-check, trace each side back to its inputs. If both sides share an input fitted to the target, the check is void and must be withdrawn by a correction record.

\emph{R2, corrections as records.} Four results (D1265, D1273, D1274, D1275) received six correction notes that night (D1265a/b, D1273a, D1274a, D1275a/b). Each correction is its own note naming the original. In the machine's memory a correction now supersedes the original automatically, so a withdrawn claim cannot be learned as evidence.

\emph{R3, evaluate where used.} A response $dn_s/d\ln C$ that varies by about 40\% across the band had been taken at the wrong point. The value used, 0.0117, is the slope near $C\approx1.12\times10^{-6}$. At the benchmark a fresh symmetric difference gives 0.0133, so the value used was 12\% low (D1265a, refined in D1265b). The first correction (0.0135, from a wide quadratic fit) was itself 1.3\% high. This shows the rule applies to corrections too. \textbf{Rule:} evaluate any locally linear coefficient at the point where it is applied, and state that point.

\emph{R4, predict before testing, and grade the risk.} A rule (``an optimal lattice flips with scale iff it is not self-dual'') led to a prediction for E6 and E7, logged in a derivation note (D1278) before the computation (D1282) and then confirmed~\cite{SSD}. Two caveats apply. First, the prediction was logged in a committed note, not in a SHA-256 lock. Second, the test compared E6 and E7 only with their own duals. By Poisson duality, any lattice that is not isometric to its dual must swap with it at the self-dual scale (D1282, steps 1--2), so the outcome was nearly certain once the lattices were known not to be self-dual. The riskier claim, that E6 and E7 beat every standard family in $d=6,7$, was locked separately later that morning (PREREG\_D1286). \textbf{Rule:} log the prediction before the test, and state whether a known identity already implies it. A prediction that cannot fail checks the arithmetic, not the rule.

\emph{R5, print raw.} A \texttt{round(x,6)} printed $1.25\times10^{-6}$ as ``$1\times10^{-6}$'' and sent us chasing a non-existent bug. (This incident is from the working session and is not recorded in the archive's notes.) \textbf{Rule:} use scientific notation for any quantity below $10^{-3}$ in check output.

\emph{R6, grade the machine.} A local model's proposals went through four phases: tautologies, falsifiable but wrong, fixation on one parameter, and category errors~\cite{CM}. The first three were addressed by changing the prompt, the memory and the deploy path, not the model. The category errors persisted in part after two prompt rules aimed at them. None of the 10 proposals graded before this note was promoted, nor any of the 16 graded by the end of the morning~\cite{CM}. \textbf{Rule:} until machine proposals survive independent checks at a steady rate, a grader stays in the loop, and every deploy is verified by checksum on arrival.

\section{What did not change}
The v1 core stands: lock first, write deviations before reruns, report failures, and score post-hoc models only on future data. The audit re-checked the computed results that existed when it ran (D1265a, D1272--D1279) by independent methods: closed forms, Poisson identities, brute force against formulas, and fresh exact runs. Results computed after the audit (D1280, D1282) carry only their own internal cross-checks. The audit found no arithmetic error. The corrections concerned interpretation, unsourced data inputs, and the point at which a coefficient was evaluated.
