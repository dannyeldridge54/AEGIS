\begin{abstract}
This note accompanies the corrected SERAPIS text (v3.3, included in this record) and reports every test run since v3.2 (doi:10.5281/zenodo.24289118, 16 November 2026). Each test was locked (rules and pass criteria committed with a checksum) before it was computed; nothing below was tuned after seeing a result. In short: the full inflation prediction sits within $1.6\sigma$ of every current spectral-index measurement and below every tensor bound, but its $n_s$ is a fit, so only $r\simeq5.4\times10^{-4}$ (10D) or $1.07\times10^{-3}$ (6D) and $\alpha_s\simeq-9.7\times10^{-4}$ are tests. The late-universe ``reverse thread'' beats $\Lambda$CDM on full Planck + DESI DR2 + three supernova sets in two independent Boltzmann codes, but trails the two-parameter $w_0w_a$ fit, and a proposed coupling between the thread and dark matter was \emph{not} detected. Exact tribimaximal-2 neutrino mixing at the model's two vacua is excluded by JUNO. Under strict counting (predictions locked before computation, nothing chosen after seeing the same data, nothing inherited from earlier physics) the score is 4 pass, 2 tension, 1 fail. None of these tests used data released after the prediction was made; such tests (LiteBIRD, CMB-S4, JUNO six-year, DUNE, DESI DR3) are still ahead.
\end{abstract}

The thread improves on $\Lambda$CDM in both codes, but only DES-Y5 shows a clear gain after the parameter penalty, and $w_0w_a$ does better everywhere. A coupling between the thread and dark matter, which on compressed CMB data had closed this gap (D1710), was tested on full Planck under a locked rule and \textbf{not detected}: best-fit couplings $b_A=+0.004$, $+0.020$, $-0.041$, consistent with zero (D1725, verdict \emph{fix\_fails}). The ``negative space'' derived from that coupling (an effective $\Lambda$ that turns negative above $z_\dagger$; D1716--D1717) therefore remains a derivation without data support. A minimiser failure on two sets was caught by a nested-model guard and is reported, not rerun. $S_8$ of the thread fits (0.808--0.813) agrees with KiDS-Legacy ($0.815^{+0.016}_{-0.021}$) within $0.5\sigma$ and with CMB lensing (0.828 $\pm$ 0.012) within $1.7\sigma$.

\section{Neutrinos and flavour}
Exact tribimaximal-2 mixing at the two vacua (charged leptons at $\rho$, neutrinos at $i$) gives $\sin^2\theta_{12}=0.3408$ (D1598--D1599, locked before the JUNO comparison, although JUNO's first data were already public); JUNO measured $0.3092\pm0.0087$, a $+3.6\sigma$ exclusion (D1652). \textbf{That assignment failed.} Because TM2 is a known mixing pattern, it tests the model's embedding rather than a new relation, and it is counted as inherited. The surviving assignment is TM1 with modular $S_4$ at the same two vacua (adopted after JUNO, so scored as post-hoc): $\sin^2\theta_{12}=0.3184$, $+1.35\sigma$ from the post-JUNO global value $0.3085\pm0.0073$; $\delta_{CP}$ on the data-chosen branch runs from $272.5^\circ$ to $293.7^\circ$ over $\sin^2\theta_{23}\in[0.51,0.59]$, inside the T2K+NOvA $1\sigma$ range $[214^\circ,313^\circ]$. JUNO's six-year precision ($\pm0.0015$) would put TM1 about $6\sigma$ from today's central value if that value holds: the sharpest near-term test of the flavour sector. Koide $Q=2/3$ holds to $-0.4\sigma$ (an inherited relation). $\sum m_\nu=0.0587$ eV (normal ordering, $m_1=0$ assumed) is below the DESI DR2 + CMB bound.

\section{Matter genesis and light elements}
Helium and deuterium agree with measurements once the nuclear-rate theory error is included (PRIMAT convention, chosen in advance): $Y_p$ $+1.06\sigma$ (LBT 2026), D/H $-1.66\sigma$ (Cooke et al.\ 2018; $-2.62\sigma$ with the observational error alone). Because the Weave leaves standard nucleosynthesis unchanged ($\Delta N_\mathrm{eff}\approx0$ for the brane-separated dark sector), these are \emph{inherited} agreements, not evidence for the model. The lithium problem is reproduced (predicted $^7$Li/H $=4.35\times10^{-10}$ vs the Spite plateau $1.6\pm0.3$, $3.5\sigma$ with the theory error) and \textbf{not solved}. The dark-sector branch with a 12.8 GeV dark baryon from chemical equilibrium survives current xenon limits (D1676--D1677).

\section{Dark matter: the wino gamma line}
Against the H.E.S.S. Inner Galaxy Survey 2026 line limits (arXiv:2608.07234), at the three quoted masses: 440 GeV passes for every halo; 1 TeV is in tension for the benchmark Einasto halo (maximum allowed fraction 0.139 vs predicted 0.124--0.205) and passes for a cored Burkert halo (0.286); 2.9 TeV fails on relic density (a thermal wino that heavy over-closes the universe). The verdict depends on the halo core, as found before (D1125, D1322).

\section{Strict evidence count}
All quantities are collected in an Evidence Atlas (included as CSV and JSON), generated by code from the live toolkits under rules locked before it was built (\texttt{PREREG\_EVIDENCE\_ATLAS} and its deviation note, which made the count stricter). A row counts as evidence only if it is a prediction that could fail, was locked before computation, was not chosen after seeing the same data, and is not inherited from earlier physics; one row per independent test.

The four strict passes are: $r$ below every bound; the thread beating $\Lambda$CDM after the parameter penalty; $S_8$ of the thread fits; and the 12.8 GeV dark baryon surviving direct-detection limits. Three of them are bounds or comparisons, not measured-value matches. The strict failure is the thread--dark-matter coupling; the inherited failures are exact TM2 mixing and lithium-7. No test has yet used data released after its prediction. This count is meant to be easy to check and hard to inflate.
