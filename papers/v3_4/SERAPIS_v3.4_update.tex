\begin{abstract}
This note accompanies the corrected SERAPIS text (v3.3, included in this record) and reports every test run since v3.2 (doi:10.5281/zenodo.24289118, 16 November 2026). Each new test was locked (rules and pass criteria committed with a checksum) before it was computed; where a rule was changed afterwards, the change was written down first and is cited. Some choices were made after seeing data and are labelled as such: the spectral-index tilt is fitted, the TM1 neutrino assignment was adopted after JUNO excluded TM2, and the Planck priors of the coupled fits were widened after crashed runs. In short: the species-plateau realisation lies within $1.8\sigma$ of every current $n_s$ measurement and below every tensor bound, with $n_s$ fitted. The late-universe ``reverse thread'' improves on $\Lambda$CDM on full Planck + DESI DR2 + three supernova sets in two Boltzmann codes, but meaningfully (after the parameter penalty) only on DES-Y5, and the two-parameter $w_0w_a$ fit does better everywhere; a proposed coupling between the thread and dark matter was \emph{not} detected. Exact tribimaximal-2 mixing at the model's vacua is excluded by JUNO. Under strict counting the score is 1 pass, 2 tension, 1 fail, with one test open. No test has yet used data released after its prediction.
\end{abstract}

\section{What changed in the text (v3.3)}
The included v3.3 text corrects v3.2 without changing any prediction: the proton-stability sentence (an anomaly-free non-$R$ symmetry \emph{can} forbid the dangerous operators, but the modular centre cannot supply it; D657a); a limitations paragraph on which modulus can roll (D925--D937); extended caveats on the plateau's origin and its one-loop sign conditions (D941--D988); and an independent-reproduction paragraph (separately written code reproduces $n_s$ and $r$ on all branches and $\alpha_s$ to 0.2\% after a convergence test; D953--D990). Full list: \texttt{CHANGELOG\_v3.3.md}.

\section{Early universe: the inflation prediction against the newest CMB data}
Exact Mukhanov--Sasaki results for the species-plateau realisation with the Ushas tilt (locked runs PREREG\_SPECIES32/P3, D1523), $N_*$ fixed by reheating ($\simeq 50.4$--$50.6$). These lie inside the frozen v3.2 prediction-sheet bands ($r=4.6$--$5.7\times10^{-4}$, $\alpha_s=-(8.6$--$9.9)\times10^{-4}$); the $\alpha_s=-9.88\times10^{-4}$ quoted in the v3.3 text belongs to the SERAPIS-E branch at its own $N_*$, a different realisation:

Pulls of $n_s$ (10D): Planck 2018 $+1.4\sigma$; ACT DR6 $+0.5$; P-ACT $-0.1$; P-ACT-LB $-1.1$; P-ACT-LB2 $-1.5$; SPT-3G D1 $+1.8$; CMB-SPA $+0.8$; CMB-SPA+DESI $-0.8$ (sets overlap; never summed). $r$ passes BK18 ($<0.036$), P-ACT+BK18 ($<0.038$) and PR4+BK18 ($<0.032$). \textbf{Label:} $n_s$ is fitted (the tilt was chosen to give 0.97065), so it is not evidence. The genuine predictions are $r$, $\alpha_s$ and the factor-two ratio $r_{6D}/r_{10D}$, but they are hard to test: forecasts give $\sigma(\alpha_s)\approx2\times10^{-3}$ (about twice the predicted size), and $r=5.4\times10^{-4}$ is below the planned detection thresholds of LiteBIRD and CMB-S4. The near-term test is therefore an \emph{upper limit}: a detection of $r>1.1\times10^{-3}$, or a limit $r<2.4\times10^{-4}$, would exclude the model.

\section{Late universe: the reverse thread on full Planck}
The thread (one extra parameter) was fitted with full Planck 2018, DESI DR2 BAO and three supernova compilations, in CAMB (D1712) and in a patched CLASS v3.3.0 validated against an independent background solver (D1713--D1715, D1725):

The thread improves on $\Lambda$CDM in both codes, but only DES-Y5 shows a meaningful gain after the parameter penalty ($\Delta$AIC $<-2$), and $w_0w_a$ does better everywhere. Register item C8 (data with the CMB prefer phantom-like behaviour at high redshift), recorded as ``fixed (candidate)'' on compressed CMB data (D1710), is therefore OPEN again. A coupling between the thread and dark matter, which on compressed CMB data had closed this gap (D1710), was tested on full Planck under a locked rule and \textbf{not detected}: best-fit couplings $b_A=+0.004$, $+0.020$, $-0.041$ with no sign of a detection (D1725, verdict \emph{fix\_fails}). Caveats: no error bars on $b_A$ were computed, and on Pantheon+ and Union3 the coupled minimiser did not converge (it finished above the uncoupled thread, which it contains), so those two values are not reliable best fits. The ``negative space'' derived from that coupling (an effective $\Lambda$ that turns negative above $z_\dagger$; D1716--D1717) therefore remains a derivation without data support: its original $z_\dagger=2.93$ used $b=-0.074$, and among the full-Planck fits only Union3 ($b=-0.041$) gives a finite $z_\dagger\approx3.7$. A minimiser failure on two sets was caught by a nested-model guard and is reported, not rerun. $S_8$ of the thread fits (0.808--0.813) agrees with KiDS-Legacy ($0.815^{+0.016}_{-0.021}$) within $0.5\sigma$ and with CMB lensing (0.828 $\pm$ 0.012) within $1.7\sigma$; $\Lambda$CDM does equally well (0.813--0.815), so $S_8$ does not discriminate.

\section{Neutrinos and flavour}
Exact tribimaximal-2 mixing at the two vacua (charged leptons at $\rho$, neutrinos at $i$) gives $\sin^2\theta_{12}=0.3408$ (D1598--D1599, locked before the JUNO comparison, although JUNO's first data were already public); JUNO measured $0.3092\pm0.0087$, a $+3.6\sigma$ exclusion ($+4.6\sigma$ with JUNO + NuFIT combined; D1652). \textbf{That assignment failed.} The earlier flavour paper's TM2 statement (doi:10.5281/zenodo.24377945) is superseded by this result. Because TM2 is a known mixing pattern, it tests the model's embedding rather than a new relation, and it is counted as inherited. The surviving assignment is TM1 with modular $S_4$ at the same two vacua (adopted after JUNO, so scored as post-hoc): $\sin^2\theta_{12}=0.3184$, $+1.35\sigma$ from the post-JUNO global value $0.3085\pm0.0073$; $\delta_{CP}$ on the data-chosen branch runs from $272.5^\circ$ to $293.7^\circ$ over $\sin^2\theta_{23}\in[0.51,0.59]$, inside the T2K+NOvA $1\sigma$ range $[214^\circ,313^\circ]$. JUNO's six-year precision ($\pm0.0015$) would put TM1 about $6\sigma$ from today's central value if that value holds: the sharpest near-term test of the flavour sector. Koide $Q=2/3$ holds to $-0.4\sigma$ (an inherited relation). $\sum m_\nu=0.0587$ eV (normal ordering, $m_1=0$ assumed) is below the DESI DR2 + CMB bound.

\section{Matter genesis and light elements}
Helium and deuterium agree with measurements once the nuclear-rate theory error is included (PRIMAT convention, chosen in advance): $Y_p$ $+1.06\sigma$ (LBT 2026), D/H $-1.66\sigma$ (Cooke et al.\ 2018; $-2.62\sigma$ with the observational error alone). Because the Weave leaves standard nucleosynthesis unchanged ($\Delta N_\mathrm{eff}\approx0$ for the brane-separated dark sector), these are \emph{inherited} agreements, not evidence for the model. The lithium problem is reproduced (predicted $^7$Li/H $=4.35\times10^{-10}$ vs the Spite plateau $1.6\pm0.3$, $3.5\sigma$ with the theory error) and \textbf{not solved}. The dark-sector branch gives a 12.8 GeV dark baryon from chemical equilibrium (D1676). Whether it survives direct detection is \emph{open}: the xenon limit used in D1677 is an unsourced bracket, the LZ curve at 13 GeV is not yet sourced (D1678), and survival depends on a free portal coupling.

\section{Dark matter: the wino gamma line}
Against the H.E.S.S. Inner Galaxy Survey 2026 line limits (arXiv:2608.07234), at the three quoted masses: 440 GeV passes for every halo; 1 TeV is in tension for the benchmark Einasto halo (maximum allowed fraction 0.139 vs predicted 0.124--0.205) and passes for a cored Burkert halo (0.286); 2.9 TeV fails both the line limit (predicted fraction 1.04--1.73 against allowed 0.116 for Einasto, 0.239 for Burkert) and the relic density (a thermal wino that heavy would supply all the dark matter or more). The verdict depends on the halo core, as found before (D1125, D1322). These wino masses are benchmarks and the thermal wino of SERAPIS-E was adopted post-hoc (D784), so these rows are not counted as evidence. The engine also reports two standard checks: the 1 TeV wino is above the ATLAS disappearing-track limit (670 GeV), and the SLy maximum neutron-star mass (2.05 M$_\odot$) is within $1\sigma$ of PSR J0740+6620 ($2.08\pm0.07$).
