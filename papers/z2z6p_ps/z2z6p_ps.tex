\title{The global Pati--Salam model on $T^6/(\mathbb{Z}_2\times\mathbb{Z}_6'\times\Omega\mathcal{R})$:\\ independent reconstruction, uniqueness in its class, and a settled consistency and phenomenology ledger}
\author{Danny Eldridge}

\affiliation{Independent researcher, Oklahoma City, Oklahoma, USA}
\date{22 November 2026}
\begin{abstract}
We rebuild from scratch, using only the formulas published in Ref.~\cite{HRS}, the five-stack globally consistent supersymmetric Pati--Salam model of intersecting D6-branes on the $\mathbb{Z}_2\times\mathbb{Z}_6'$ orientifold with discrete torsion (AAA lattice, $\eta_{\Omega\mathcal R}=-1$). Our code reproduces the bulk and twisted RR tadpole cancellation, the full chiral spectrum, and the $15\times5$ Green--Schwarz coupling matrix of Ref.~\cite{HRS}. The matrix also fixes an ordering convention that the published tables leave implicit. A pre-registered exhaustive scan shows that, among configurations with equal-volume visible stacks and at most two hidden stacks, this model's chiral spectrum is the \emph{only} three-family Pati--Salam spectrum, up to left--right exchange. The model passes the K-theory constraint on all orientifold-invariant probe cycles. We then settle a phenomenology ledger:
\begin{itemize}
\item The $SU(2)$ one-loop threshold expression of Ref.~\cite{HRS} matches its own stated asymptote only after two coefficient corrections. Its printed isotropic unification condition follows from neither reading.
\item With that consistent reading, matching the measured couplings forces $M_s\sim10^{13}$\,GeV and thresholds of at least $77\%$ of tree level, so the matching is not under perturbative control.
\item Breaking Pati--Salam to the Standard Model is obstructed perturbatively.
\item The hidden sector is consistent with $\Delta N_{\rm eff}$ bounds only if its chiral multiplets are massive or never thermalised.
\end{itemize}
\end{abstract}
\keywords{intersecting D-branes, orientifolds, Pati--Salam, K-theory, gauge coupling unification}

\section{Introduction}
Ref.~\cite{HRS} found that, on $T^6/(\mathbb{Z}_2\times\mathbb{Z}_6'\times\Omega\mathcal R)$ with discrete torsion, local MSSM-like and left--right symmetric configurations admit no global supersymmetric completion, while a global five-stack Pati--Salam (PS) model exists. Rigid fractional cycles remove adjoint matter, the visible stacks share one bulk cycle (tree-level unification), and only one generation has perturbative Yukawa couplings. This note reproduces that model independently, asks whether it is isolated, and closes every consistency and phenomenology item to an explicit verdict.

Every computation was pre-registered: the rules and their SHA-256 hashes were committed before running, and deviations were logged before any rerun~\cite{repo}. All claims were then audited by an independent referee pass. The corrections that pass produced are incorporated and listed in the repository.

\section{Reconstruction}
Fractional three-cycles are $\Pi=\tfrac14(\Pi^{\rm bulk}+\sum_{k,\alpha}\Pi^{\mathbb Z_2^{(k)}}_\alpha)$. The bulk wrapping numbers are $X=n^1n^2n^3-m^1m^2m^3-\sum_{\rm cyc}n^im^jm^k$ and $Y=\sum_{\rm cyc}(n^in^jm^k+n^im^jm^k)$. Supersymmetry requires $Y=0$ and $2X+Y>0$, and the bulk tadpole is $\sum_x N_x(2X_x+Y_x)=32$.

We transcribed the complete exceptional-wrapping table of Ref.~\cite{HRS} (9 parity classes $\times$ 4 displacement columns) and the orientifold images on AAA. Two readings were needed, both fixed before the control run.
\begin{itemize}
\item In block 3, the printed $\alpha_1=1+\sigma^k(4-2\sigma^k)$ collides with $\alpha_3$. We read it as $1+\sigma^k(4-2\sigma^j)$, which reproduces the listed labels in all columns.
\item In the type-II entries, the constant ``1'' is read as the $\mathbb Z_2^{(i)}$ eigenvalue sign $z_0=(-1)^{\tau^{\mathbb Z_2^{(i)}}}$. The literal reading fails the control: 7 twisted conditions are violated.
\end{itemize}
With $\eta_{(k)}=-1$:
\begin{itemize}
\item the bulk tadpole equals 32;
\item all 15 non-trivial twisted conditions vanish (the other 9 are identically satisfied);
\item the intersection numbers reproduce the chiral-spectrum table of Ref.~\cite{HRS} entry by entry: $ab{=}1$, $ab'{=}2$, $ac{=}{-1}$, $ac'{=}{-2}$, $bc{=}1$, $bd{=}1$, $bd'{=}{-3}$, $be'{=}{-1}$, $cd{=}{-1}$, $cd'{=}3$, $ce'{=}1$, with all others zero.
\end{itemize}
The symmetric and antisymmetric net chiralities, $\chi^{\rm S}=\chi^{\rm A}=\tfrac12\Pi_x\circ\Pi_{x'}$, vanish for all stacks.

\paragraph{Green--Schwarz matrix and ordering convention.} The tables leave implicit whether the $(j,k)$ tori of sector $i$ are taken in cyclic or increasing order. The two choices differ only in sector 2, and the tadpoles and the spectrum do not distinguish them. The Green--Schwarz couplings $N_xc^{(k),\alpha}_x$ do. Increasing order reproduces all 75 entries of the matrix in Ref.~\cite{HRS}, while cyclic order fails on four rows. The rank is 5, so all five $U(1)$s are massive.

\paragraph{K-theory.} We enumerated the fractional cycles with $|n|,|m|\le3$ and $Y=0$ that equal their own orientifold image. Up to orientation there are 256 supersymmetric ones, and the enumeration is saturated: it is unchanged at $|n|,|m|\le5$. They form a superset of the $USp$ probes; their total matches the $144+112$ invariant branes of Ref.~\cite{HRS}, though our split by $X$ differs from the $USp/SO$ split. Image terms cancel for invariant probes. For every probe, $\sum_x N_x\,\Pi_x\circ\Pi_p\in\{0,\pm4,\pm8\}$, which is even, so the K-theory constraint~\cite{Uranga} holds.

\paragraph{FI terms.} The twisted-sector FI parameters of the anomalous $U(1)$s are linear in the $\mathbb Z_2$ twisted moduli, with exactly the Green--Schwarz coefficients~\cite{BCMS}. At the orbifold point they vanish, so the configuration is supersymmetric, and the rank-5 coupling matrix freezes five combinations of twisted moduli.

\section{Uniqueness of the chiral spectrum in its class}
We scanned all visible triples $a(4),b(2),c(2)$ on a common supersymmetric bulk cycle; the tadpole budget forces $2X+Y=2$, giving 36 wrappings $\times$ 256 fractional labels. Each triple had to give three left- and three right-handed families, with no stack equal to its own image. Each was then completed by at most two hidden stacks with $2X+Y\le14$, solving all tadpole conditions exactly.

The scan finds 5{,}184 raw (at most 864 distinct) visible triples. Every one is globally completable, and the published model is among them. Comparing every distinct-by-cycle model with no cap, on the signature $(ab,ab',ac,ac',bc,bc',\chi^{\rm S/A},$ hidden ranks, per-stack visible--hidden $|\chi|)$, yields exactly two signatures. They are the left--right mirror pair of the published spectrum: hidden $U(2)^2$, $bc=\pm1$, and visible--hidden $|\chi|=(0,5,5)$ for $(a,b,c)$. This agrees with and extends the statement in Ref.~\cite{HRS} that its 27 rigid three-stack configurations share one chiral spectrum. Non-chiral spectra were not compared. The published six-stack PS model on this background~\cite{HS13} has three hidden stacks and lies outside this class, so uniqueness holds relative to the two-hidden-stack bound.

\section{Phenomenology ledger}
\paragraph{Beta functions.} The beta-function formula of Ref.~\cite{HRS}, with the multiplet counts of its spectrum tables, gives $b_{SU(4)}=-2$, $b_{SU(2)_{L,R}}=+6$, $b_{SU(2)_d}=+28$ and $b_{SU(2)_e}=+2$.

\paragraph{Thresholds.} We evaluated the one-loop gauge kinetic functions with exact $\vartheta_1/\eta$. The $SU(4)$ expression matches its stated asymptote to $10^{-7}$. The $SU(2)_{L,R}$ expression matches its stated asymptote ($v_3$ slope $-1/16\pi$, no $v_m$ term) only after two changes: $\tfrac1{2\pi}\to\tfrac1{2\pi^2}$, and the sign of the $\tfrac1{4\pi^2}\ln\eta(iv_3)$ term. We adopt this consistent reading. The condition $f_a=f_b$ at $v_1=v_2$ is then $v=\tfrac14v_3+\tfrac{7\ln2}{\pi}$. The printed $4v_3+\tfrac{168\ln2}{\pi}$ follows from neither this reading nor the one-change reading ($\tfrac54v_3+\ldots$). We flag this for the authors.

\paragraph{String scale.} Ref.~\cite{HRS} gives $4\pi/g^2=2\pi\,{\rm Re}\,f$, $\alpha^{-1}_{\rm tree}=3.53\times10^{-4}\,M_{\rm Pl}/M_s$ (reduced $M_{\rm Pl}$), and one-loop shifts $\delta\alpha^{-1}=2\pi\,{\rm Re}\,\delta f$. The one-loop MSSM unified value $\alpha_X^{-1}=24.3$ would need $M_s\simeq3.5\times10^{13}$\,GeV, $570\times$ below $M_{\rm GUT}$.

This test assumes PS$\to$SM breaking at $M_s$ (see below) with heavy exotics, so it is conditional on an unspecified breaking mechanism. We matched the three measured couplings at $M_s$ against $(v_1,v_2,v_3,g_s)$, which gives a one-parameter family. We minimised the threshold-to-tree ratio over that family, requiring $v_i\ge1$ and $g_s<1$. With the consistent reading, the minimum is $0.82$ (reduced $M_{\rm Pl}$; $0.77$ non-reduced), reached on the boundary $v_2=1$ with $M_s\approx8\times10^{12}$\,GeV. The pre-registered control criterion ($<0.3$) fails. (The one-change reading would give $0.22$--$0.28$.) The full matching has no solution with $v_1=v_2$; this is distinct from the near-isotropy of the condition $f_a=f_b$ alone. If instead $M_s=M_{\rm GUT}$ and only the MSSM is light, $\alpha_s(M_Z)=0.117$ at one loop. With the PS-vector-like states light (1\,TeV), the couplings become non-perturbative.

\paragraph{Breaking Pati--Salam.} Recombination requires deformation moduli, i.e.\ adjoint multiplets~\cite{HRS}. Stacks $a$ and $c$ are rigid, and the spectrum contains no $(\mathbf4,\mathbf1,\mathbf2)$ Higgs, so PS$\to$SM breaking is obstructed perturbatively. The open routes are non-perturbative couplings, recombination through the flexible hidden stack $d$, or leaving the rigid-cycle class.

\paragraph{Discrete symmetries and Yukawas.} The five massive $U(1)$s leave discrete remnants $\mathbb Z_n$, which D-brane instantons respect~\cite{HS13,BIZn}. The survival condition~\cite{HS13} is $\Pi^{\rm even}\circ\sum_xk_xN_x\Pi_x\equiv0$ mod $n$ for every $\Omega\mathcal R$-even cycle. We test it on the full integral even lattice: 7{,}223 independent test vectors from $\Pi+\Pi'$ of all fractional cycles with $|n|,|m|\le2$, plus the invariant cycles. A test on the Green--Schwarz coefficients alone covers only the necessary conditions and wrongly admits a $\mathbb Z_8$. $\mathbb Z_2$ and $\mathbb Z_4$ survive. Under the surviving $\mathbb Z_4$, the perturbative coupling ($ac\cdot bc\cdot ab$) and the generation-2,3 block ($ac'\cdot bc\cdot ab'$) are allowed, so instantons can generate the latter with suppression $S\simeq5.6$ and $11.8$ (estimate). The mixed couplings $ac\cdot bc\cdot ab'$ and $ac'\cdot bc\cdot ab$ are forbidden to all instanton orders. The Yukawa matrices are therefore block-diagonal, $1\oplus2$, so the first generation cannot mix with the other two without additional Higgs fields or higher-dimensional operators with $\mathbb Z_4$-charged vevs. This sharpens the statement in Ref.~\cite{HRS} that only one generation has perturbative Yukawas.

\paragraph{Hidden sector.} All $U(1)$s are massive. The hidden $SU(2)_d\times SU(2)_e$ are infrared free. If the whole hidden sector is thermal and decouples at $M_s$, the six vectors alone give $\Delta N_{\rm eff}\approx0.11$. Adding the massless hidden-only chiral fermions gives $0.56$, and all massless hidden states give $0.82$. The bound $\Delta N_{\rm eff}\lesssim0.3$ therefore requires hidden-sector masses or a non-thermal hidden sector.

\section{Conclusion}
The global Pati--Salam model of Ref.~\cite{HRS} is a fully consistent string vacuum (tadpoles, K-theory, anomalous $U(1)$ masses, FI terms), and its chiral spectrum is the only three-family PS spectrum in its class. It is therefore a sharp, rigid target rather than one point in a landscape. Its obstacles are physical rather than formal. Gauge-coupling matching needs an intermediate string scale and one-loop corrections comparable to tree level; there is no perturbative route from Pati--Salam to the Standard Model; the hidden matter must be massive or non-thermal; and a surviving $\mathbb Z_4$ makes the Yukawa matrices block-diagonal, forbidding first-generation mixing. These define the next steps: the $SU(2)$ thresholds from first principles, hidden-sector mass terms, and a search beyond rigid cycles for a PS-breaking sector.
