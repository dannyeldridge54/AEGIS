GLOSSARY ADDITIONS for the schoolbook (two columns: Term | Meaning). Insert only the rows whose term is not already in
the glossary table (check first). Each meaning says what it is, how we know it, and how it connects to the twist field.

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Twist field (T) | The one field the whole theory is built on: T = x + i·y. x is its size (the dilaton), y its angle. It comes from loop quantum gravity's Barbero–Immirzi number turned into a field. Its energy drives inflation early and is dark energy today. |
| Dilaton (x) | The "size" half of the twist field. How stretched the field is; written x = e^{√(2/3)φ}. Rolling in x is what drives inflation, the cliff and the tail. |
| Inflaton (φ) | The size of the twist field measured in natural (Planck) units, so that its motion has ordinary energy. The field that drives inflation. |
| Twist angle (y) | The "angle" half of the twist field. It talks to matter only through how fast it turns (Nieh–Yan coupling). A spinning angle tilts the balance of matter over antimatter. |
| Barbero–Immirzi parameter (γ) | A number in loop quantum gravity that sets the smallest piece of area (calculated ≈ 0.24–0.27 from black-hole entropy). The Twist promotes it to a field. |
| Torsion | A twisting of spacetime, as well as bending (Einstein–Cartan gravity). Spinning particles source it. It supplies the twist angle at the quantum-gravity level; too small to measure directly today. |
| Kähler potential (K) | The rule for how "far apart" two values of the field are — the shape of the ground it moves on. In the Twist, K = −3 ln(T + T̄), fixed by the base theory. |
| The 3 | The number in K = −3 ln(T + T̄). It alone sets the slope √(2/3) that appears in inflation (n_s, r) and in today's dark energy. Fixed by the base theory, not fitted. |
| No-scale supergravity (α = 1) | The kind of supergravity the Twist uses. "No-scale" means the field's ground has a built-in flat direction; α = 1 is the case the 3 gives. Known to produce Starobinsky-like inflation. |
| Superpotential (W) | In supergravity, the "recipe" function the energy landscape comes from. In the Twist, W = √3·m·S·F(T). |
| Landscape (V) | The potential energy of the field at each value — the hills and valleys it rolls on. In the Twist, V = m²|F(T)|²/(4x²). |
| Plateau | The high, nearly flat part of the landscape where the field starts. Its energy drives inflation. |
| Cliff | The steep drop in the landscape (set by n ≈ 127) where inflation ends and the field's energy turns into motion, then heat. |
| Tail | The long gentle slope after the cliff. The energy left there is today's dark energy. |
| e-fold (N) | One growth of space by a factor e ≈ 2.718. The CMB scales left the horizon about 55 e-folds before inflation ended. |
| Spectral tilt (n_s) | Whether large or small ripples in the early universe are slightly stronger. The Twist predicts 0.963–0.965; measured 0.964 (Planck + DESI) to 0.973 (with ACT/SPT). |
| Tensor-to-scalar ratio (r) | How strong inflation's gravitational waves are compared with its density ripples. The Twist predicts 0.0034–0.0040; LiteBIRD can measure it. |
| Reheating (T_RH) | The moment after inflation when the universe fills with heat. In the Twist it is calculated: about 2–4×10¹⁴ GeV. |
| Instant preheating | How the Twist reheats: as the inflaton races past the cliff it suddenly creates particles (χ) that decay into ordinary matter and light. |
| Hidden axion (σ) | A second angle-like field in a hidden sector. At the cliff it pulls on the twist angle and gives it the kick that makes matter; then it decays away. |
| Hidden SU(8) | A hidden force with 8 "colours", like the strong force. When it condenses it leaves exactly the term that links the twist angle to the hidden axion — and 8 is the twist angle's own period. |
| Holomorphy | A rule of supergravity: the recipe W can depend on T but not on its mirror image T̄. It forces any angle-dependent energy to fade as e^{−Ax}, which is why the kick needs the hidden axion. |
| Baryon asymmetry | The small excess of matter over antimatter — about 6 extra protons and neutrons for every 10 billion particles of light. Everything we are made of. |
| Sphaleron | A process in the Standard Model that turns an excess of leptons into an excess of baryons (28/79 of it). |
| Weinberg operator | The tiny interaction that gives neutrinos their masses. In the hot early universe it lets lepton number change, turning the angle's spin bias into a lepton excess. |
| R-parity violation | A small coupling that lets heavy supersymmetric partner particles decay. The Twist needs it (between about 10⁻⁵ and 10⁻³) so those partners are not left over. |
| Isocurvature | Patches with slightly more or less of one ingredient (here, matter) than the average. The hidden axion would make some; the Twist's amount is about 100 times below Planck's limit. |
| Dark-matter coupling (β) | An optional energy exchange between dark energy and dark matter. The data slightly prefer β ≈ 0.03, but the Twist's own gravity-only dark matter requires β = 0. |
| Thawing dark energy | Dark energy that was frozen for most of history and has only recently started to roll, so w rises slightly above −1. The Twist's tail is thawing: w₀ ≈ −0.90. |
| w₀, w_a | The two numbers used to describe dark energy: w₀ is its pressure-to-energy ratio today (−1 for a cosmological constant), w_a how fast it changes. The Twist's own version predicts w₀ ≈ −0.90 and w_a between about −0.15 and 0. |
| fσ₈ | How fast clumps of matter are growing, as surveys measure it. The pure Twist predicts about 3% slower than standard cosmology. |
| S₈ | How clumpy matter is today. The pure Twist predicts 0.793, slightly below standard cosmology's 0.798. |
| E_G | A ratio comparing how light is bent by matter (lensing) with how galaxies move. The Twist predicts it about 3.5–5% above standard cosmology. |
| BAO | Baryon acoustic oscillations: a fixed ruler in the galaxy distribution, left by sound waves in the early universe. DESI uses it to measure the expansion history. |
| Δχ² | How much better (negative) or worse one model fits data than another. −4 is a mild preference; the Twist's best results are −4 to −15. |
| Pre-registration (locked rule) | Writing a test's exact rules and pass/fail lines, fixing them with a hash and a dated commit, before seeing the data — so results cannot be bent after the fact. |
| Look-elsewhere effect | When many versions are tried on the same data, one will look good by chance; the significance must be corrected for the number tried. |
