## The Twist from start to finish

### The theory in one paragraph

Everything in the Twist comes from one field, called **T**. It was born in quantum gravity, where space itself is made of tiny linked pieces. T has two halves that always move together: a **size** half (how stretched the field is) and an **angle** half (the twist). One short equation sets the ground they move on, and one number in it — a **3** — fixes the shape of everything that follows. Early on, T sits on a high, flat **plateau**: its energy makes space grow incredibly fast (**inflation**). Then it falls off a **cliff**: inflation ends, the universe fills with heat, and the twisting angle gets a **kick** from a hidden partner field. That kick leaves slightly more matter than antimatter — the matter we are made of. In the same heat, a heavy **dark-matter** particle that feels only gravity is made. Finally T slides onto a long, gentle **tail**; the energy still stored there is today's **dark energy**, which is just starting to change. Three things work together the whole way — the size, the angle and the hidden partner — on ground shaped by the 3.

### How to read each step

Every step below has the same five parts:
- **What it is** — the piece, in plain words, and its symbol.
- **How we know it** — whether it is measured, calculated from the base theory, or chosen and then tested.
- **What it does to spacetime** — how it bends, stretches or fills space.
- **How it works with the twist field** — how it joins T.
- **Joins into** — what it feeds into next.

### Step 1 — Spacetime, and the field that lives in it

**Spacetime (g).** *What it is:* the stage — three directions of space plus time, which can bend and stretch (Einstein's general relativity). *How we know it:* measured everywhere, from GPS clocks to gravitational waves. *What it does:* tells matter how to move; matter and energy tell it how to bend. On the largest scale the only thing that matters is the **scale factor a(t)** — how much space has stretched — and its growth rate, the **Hubble rate H**. *Joins into:* everything; every other piece below either fills spacetime with energy or is moved by it.

**Twist (torsion).** *What it is:* besides bending, spacetime in the Einstein–Cartan version of gravity can also *twist* as you move around a loop, like a spiral staircase. Spinning particles source it. *How we know it:* it is part of the standard Einstein–Cartan theory; its effects are far too small to measure directly in the lab today. *What it does:* nothing you can feel in ordinary matter; it matters at the quantum-gravity level, where it supplies the angle in Step 2. *Joins into:* the Barbero–Immirzi angle.

**The Barbero–Immirzi parameter (γ).** *What it is:* a number in loop quantum gravity that sets the size of the smallest possible piece of area. *How we know it:* calculated, not measured; black-hole entropy calculations give γ ≈ 0.24–0.27. *What the Twist does with it:* instead of a fixed number, the Twist lets γ become a **field** that can change in space and time. *Joins into:* the twist field T.

**The twist field T = x + i·y.** *What it is:* one complex field with two halves. **x** (the **dilaton**, or size half) says how stretched the field is; **y** (the **angle**, or twist half) says how far it has turned. *How we know it:* T is built from γ (Gates, Ketov and Yunes found the form). The halves are written x = e^{√(2/3)φ} so that φ, the **inflaton**, is the size measured in natural units (Planck units). *What it does to spacetime:* its energy fills space; when that energy is large and nearly constant, space grows exponentially (inflation); when small, it is dark energy. *Joins into:* every step below.

### Step 2 — The ground the field moves on: K = −3 ln(T + T̄)

**The Kähler potential (K).** *What it is:* the rule that says how "far" one value of T is from another — the shape of the ground T moves on. T̄ is T's mirror image (x − i·y). *How we know it:* it comes straight from the base theory (Gates–Ketov–Yunes), not from a fit. *What it does to spacetime:* indirectly everything — it sets how much energy a moving field carries, and so how fast space grows. *How it works with the field:* it makes the ground **curved**. Walking in the size direction is easy; turning the angle costs more the smaller x is. In numbers, the angle's motion is weighted by e^{−2√(2/3)φ}. That is the **kinetic mixing** λ = −2√(2/3).

**The 3.** *What it is:* the number in front of the logarithm. *How we know it:* fixed by the base theory (it is what makes the theory "no-scale" supergravity, α = 1). *What it does:* it alone sets the slope √(2/3) ≈ 0.816 that appears at every stage —
- the plateau's shape, and so the ripple tilt n_s ≈ 1 − 2/N and the gravitational-wave strength r ≈ 12/N² (N ≈ 55 is the number of e-folds, below);
- the tail's slope κ = √(2/3), and so how dark energy changes today.
*Joins into:* inflation (Step 6) and dark energy (Step 11).

### Step 3 — How the twist talks to ordinary matter: the Nieh–Yan coupling

**The Nieh–Yan term.** *What it is:* a piece of the gravity equations that measures the overall twist of spacetime. Multiplied by the angle y, it couples the angle to matter. *How we know it:* derived from the base theory; its strength is **c_A = √(3/8) ≈ 0.61** (calculated, not fitted). *What it does to spacetime:* nothing directly; it is a "topological" term that only matters when the angle changes. *How it works with the field:* the angle talks only to the **spin direction** of particles (the axial current J₅), and only through how **fast** it changes: c_A · (dy/dt) · J₅ / M_P. A still angle does nothing; a spinning angle acts like a gentle push that favours one spin direction. *Consequences:* no fifth force on ordinary matter, no change in how things fall, no rotation of light — the six lab "nulls" (Step 13) — but a spinning angle can tilt the balance of matter and antimatter (Step 9). *Joins into:* the kick and matter-making.

**The period of 8 and A = π/4.** *What it is:* the angle repeats itself after 8 units, so any wave-like energy of the angle goes like cos(π y / 4). The number **A = π/4** sets how fast such terms fade as the size grows: they come with a factor e^{−A x}. *How we know it:* calculated from the Nieh–Yan term (instanton counting). *Joins into:* the hidden-partner term (Step 8), where 8 shows up again as a hidden force with 8 colours.

**The angle's width F ≈ 1.56 M_P.** *What it is:* how far the field must turn for one full cycle, in Planck units. *How we know it:* calculated (Nieh–Yan period). *Joins into:* the size of the angle's energy terms.
