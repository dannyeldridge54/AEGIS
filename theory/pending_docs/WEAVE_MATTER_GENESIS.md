## Part 1. One origin (postulate P-M)
**Statement.** At the end of inflation the thread decays (reheating, T ≈ 9 × 10⁸ GeV in the Weave). That decay writes ONE asymmetry, an excess of particles over antiparticles, shared between ordinary baryons and a dark mirror baryon. Dark matter and matter are then the same "stitch" seen from the two sides of the loom. This is the known asymmetric-dark-matter (ADM) class [lit: Kaplan–Luty–Zurek 0901.4117; reviews 1305.4939, 1308.0338].

**Why it matters.** It explains the coincidence that dark matter is about 5× ordinary matter:
    Ω_DM / Ω_b = 0.1200 / 0.02237 = 5.36   (Planck)   [computed]
If both sides carry the same NUMBER excess, the dark baryon's mass is fixed:
    m_DM = 5.36 × m_p = 5.03 GeV            [computed]
Order-one sharing factors move this to 1.7 GeV (n_DM/n_B = 3) up to 15 GeV (1/3). The Weave value is 5.0 GeV only if the sharing is equal, which is assumed, not derived.

**Tests.** Low-mass direct-detection searches cover the 1–10 GeV window (DarkSide-50, CRESST, SuperCDMS SNOLAB, TESSERACT). Because the dark matter is asymmetric, there is no annihilation signal today; it can collect in stars and neutron stars.

**Flags.**
- (1) "Exactly the same process" cannot be literal: dark matter does not feel light or the nuclear force, so it never builds elements. The shared part is the ORIGIN (one asymmetry), not the later chemistry.
- (2) Weave reheating at 9 × 10⁸ GeV sits just below the ~10⁹ GeV Davidson–Ibarra bound for simple thermal leptogenesis. The asymmetry mechanism needs care (resonant or non-thermal production).
- (3) A light mirror sector must not add too much radiation (ΔN_eff); it needs to stay colder than the visible sector.

## Part 2. The element ladder through time [lit — standard cosmology, Danny's picture is right]
| time | what forms | "lens" |
|---|---|---|
| ~10⁻⁶ s | protons and neutrons condense from the quark plasma | the first stitch |
| 3–20 min | Big Bang nucleosynthesis: H, D, He-3, He-4, a trace of Li-7; everything still ionized | first knot |
| 380,000 yr | recombination: electrons bind, the first neutral atoms; the CMB is released | the cloth goes clear |
| ~100–200 Myr | first stars: C, N, O up to Fe by fusion; supernovae spread them | first fire |
| ~0.3–1 Gyr onward | neutron-star mergers and rare supernovae: r-process (Au, Pt, U, Th) | the heavy weave |
| ~1 Gyr onward | AGB stars: s-process (Ba, Sr, Pb) | slow stitching |
| now and later | heavy-element content keeps growing; stars become more metal-rich each generation | |

"New elements at a certain time": the r-process in mergers briefly makes nuclei heavier than uranium. Fission fragments in old stars point to nuclei with mass number > 260 (Roederer et al., Science 2023). Superheavy elements around the predicted "island of stability" (Z ≈ 114–126) may be made in mergers. If any are long-lived they could be found in nature: in cosmic rays, in meteorites, or in the spectra of r-process-rich stars. These elements are made by EVENTS (mergers) that become more common over time, not unlocked at a fixed clock time.
