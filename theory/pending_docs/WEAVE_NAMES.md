Rule: the theory and its structure speak in **weave** words. The individual working parts carry names of sun, time, threshold and weaving figures **from cultures around the world**, one tradition per part, as an homage. SERAPIS (Greco-Egyptian) and JANUS (Roman) keep their places. Several of these figures belong to living traditions (Shinto, Hindu, Yoruba, Māori and others). ### 4. How to do it well (best practice)
1. **Ask before publishing.** For names from living traditions (Shinto, Hindu, Yoruba, Māori, Fon, Andean and Mesoamerican peoples), invite a scholar or community member from each to review the name and its one-line meaning. Change any name they find unsuitable.
2. **Credit the source.** Every name carries its culture, a short origin note and a pronunciation guide (to be added with the reviewers).
3. **Honour, never decorate.** The names label real parts with real meanings. They are not used for jokes, products or logos without permission.
4. **Leave the loom open.** New parts (new levels, new nodes) take a name from a tradition not yet represented, proposed by anyone and agreed openly ("add a thread").
5. **Keep the physics neutral.** The names are an homage; the equations, numbers and tests stay exactly as written, so the science can be checked by anyone, anywhere.
6. **Share it in many tongues.** The one line and the plain-language summary are translated by native speakers into as many languages as possible.
