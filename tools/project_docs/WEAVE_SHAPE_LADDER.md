A splitting point is a fixed point of the fold: where the shape has a corner, states localise, and the symmetry splits sectors apart.

Every stage up, the count multiplies (3 → 9 → 27 for Z₃; 4 → 16 → 64 for Z₂). Each direction keeps its own fold, potential and ladder law, as Danny said at 11:19 and 11:46.

## 4. The same splitting in the elements (established physics; the analogy, not a Weave derivation)
- **Atoms:** hydrogen's levels are degenerate n² because of a hidden 4D rotation symmetry, SO(4), whose shape is the 3-sphere. Shells fill as 2n² = 2, 8, 18, 32: splitting points of that symmetry, multiplied by spin.
- **Bonds:** carbon's four sp³ bonds point to the corners of a TETRAHEDRON (the pyramid of stage 1). Benzene's ring is a HEXAGON (the ρ-torus cell). Salts crystallise in CUBES (stage 2). Molecular shapes follow point groups, the 3D folds.
- **Nuclei:** shell closures (2, 8, 20, 28, 50, 82, 126) from the harmonic-oscillator plus spin–orbit splitting: again "splitting points" of a symmetry, but not a Farey or modular sequence (D1831).
- Honest limit: these are the same MATHEMATICS (symmetry groups and their fixed points), which is universal. They do not show that the Weave's compactification is the real one; they show the pattern is the one nature uses.

## 5. What is derived vs open
- Derived/computed: the corner counts; the multiplication rule; the per-torus assignments (from D1790–D1845); the χ compatibility of every standard fold with N = 6.
- Open: the Weave's own mixed fold and its χ; whether the 3 corners of the turnover are literally where the 3 lepton families sit (in some orbifold models, families localise at fixed points). That is a lockable test: compare the family count to the fixed points per direction.
