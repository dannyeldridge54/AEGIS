## What they look for (common to every posting read)
1. A degree **or a documented equivalent** - OU staff jobs often say experience substitutes 1:1 for the degree; Oklo says "equivalent industry experience may be considered".
2. Proof of research: published or shown work, data collection, analysis, reports.
3. Specific tools: Python, HPC, nuclear codes (MCNP, Serpent, SCALE), lab methods (HPLC, GC).
4. Regulations: NRC 10 CFR 20/61/70/71/72, DOE O 435.1, "regulations governing radioactivity in medicine".
5. Communication: technical writing, presentations, teaching and training others.
6. Working with others: collaboration across teams; mentoring.

## Cheapest real paths (no degree)
- **Ask for what the professors' grants can pay for**: research technician or student-worker hours, created by a PI. This matches "compensated to learn and work alongside you".
- **Radiation safety technologist route** (posting 3): 200 h of training plus a year under an RSO is a recognised non-degree path.
- **OU staff jobs with the equivalency clause** (posting 6 type): apply with the GitHub as the experience record.
- Free skill gaps to close: OpenMC (open-source Monte Carlo, the MCNP cousin); NRC 10 CFR Part 20 reading; a short HPLC/GC primer.
