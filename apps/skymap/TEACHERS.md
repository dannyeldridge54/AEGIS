# AEGIS Sky Map — notes for teachers

**Ages:** about 10 and up. **Time:** a 10-minute demo to a full lesson. **Needs:** a browser; a projector for whole-class use.

## A 10-minute demo
1. Open **Tour** and press **Show me** on each stop. Read the short text aloud, or let students read it.
2. At stop 4 (the galactic centre), switch **Layers → Sky picture** between visible light and infrared. Ask: *why can we see more stars in infrared?*
3. End on the Gaia map: *every bright speck is a count of stars measured by one spacecraft.*

## Ready-made activities (in the Learn panel)
- **Same sky, different light** (15 min) — compare four kinds of light on one region.
- **Planet hunt** (20 min, evening homework) — use **Tonight** with your town, find a planet outside, check again a week later.
- **Be the reviewer** (25 min) — open flagged stars in **AEGIS stars**; *before* voting, agree what evidence would convince the
  class. Download the votes as a CSV for discussion. This teaches the idea of deciding your test before seeing the result.

## Good questions to ask
- The stars in Orion look close together. Are they? (No: hundreds of light-years apart, see the tour.)
- Why does Andromeda look like it does "2.5 million years ago"?
- A star is flagged as "moving too fast". What else could make it look that way? (Wrong distance, measurement error, a companion.)

## Tips
- **A+** in the top bar makes all text larger for projectors.
- Press **Esc** to close panels. Drag to move, scroll or pinch to zoom; the sky's own buttons add a grid and full screen.
- No sign-in and nothing is collected, so it is safe for students to use on their own.
